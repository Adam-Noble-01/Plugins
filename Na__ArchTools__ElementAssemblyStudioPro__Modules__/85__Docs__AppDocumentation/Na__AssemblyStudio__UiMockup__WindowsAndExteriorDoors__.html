1What’s out of order now

I measured these on the live dialog at about 1,000 px wide with every expander shut.

  1. The first decision comes near the end. Product, Window Type and Door Type sit in OPTIONS, the fourth section, about 1,700 px down. You set the width, frame and glaze bars before you say what you’re building, and switching product rebuilds the sections above where you’re looking.
  2. Measure and its insert button are 2,000–3,000 px apart. Measure Opening sits in the tab strip at about 70 px. Create at Measurement Point is at the bottom: about 2,100 px for a window and 3,080 px for double doors. Width and Height, which Measure fills in, sit somewhere in between.
  3. Live Mode and Update are split the same way: Live Mode is top right and Update is at the bottom.
  4. The frame is in three places. Frame Thickness and Advanced Frame are in DIMENSIONS, Frame Depth and Wall Inset are in CILL & FRAME, and Frame Finish and Edge Colours are in OPTIONS.
  5. The cill is in two. Include Cill and Paint Cill are in OPTIONS, Height and Protrusion are in CILL & FRAME, and all three stay on screen with the cill switched off.
  6. DIMENSIONS is a catch-all. It holds overall size, frame, casement rails, mullions and transoms, plus four sliding-sash rail controls after the transoms. The sash horns are in OPTIONS.
  7. Glazing is split. Bar count and width are in GLAZE BARS, but Glaze Bar Inset and Glazing Thickness are inside Advanced Casement Controls.
  8. “Advanced” doesn’t mean “rare”. Casements Per Opening is a layout choice but sits next to glass thickness. Per Casement Settings is a dropdown inside a dropdown.
  9. Doors see the window’s sections first. Their own choices (swing, leaf style, handles) come last. Glaze Bars come before you’ve said whether the leaf has any glass.
  10. The door controls already carry group tags, such as Opening and Leaves, Fielded Panels and Hardware, but the renderer ignores them. Double Doors draws as one flat list of 30 controls, or 68 with the leaf overrides.
  11. Output switches are mixed in with design ones. Fuse Parts and Show Dimensions sit between Include Cill and Paint Cill.
  12. The rows are tall and in one column. Each slider takes about 41 px (label, track and number box). A default casement window is 29 controls and 2,155 px long, and double doors are 48 controls and 3,137 px.
  13. The defaults don’t match how you work. Fuse Parts is off by default but on in all 9 saved presets, and Frame Finish has been changed in all 9.
  14. The labels hide things. The “Windows” tab holds every exterior door, and Presets Gallery, the quickest way in, is a small button in the preview header.

3The new flow

Product→Size→Lights / Opening→Leaves→Glazing→Fielded panels→Frame & cill→Casements / Sashes→Hardware→Finish→Output
  • The order follows how you’d say it out loud: “a 900 by 1200 casement, two lights with a transom, three over one bars, 50 frame set back 10, painted.” Leaves and fielded panels only show for doors, Hardware only for single and double doors, and Casements/Sashes only for windows.
  • One product strip replaces the two-level Product → Type toggle. It’s one click for all six, and it’s the first thing in the panel.
  • Each topic has one home. Everything about the frame is in Frame & cill, and everything about glass is in Glazing.
  • There are three tiers inside every section. 1 is always shown. 2 sits behind More, and 3 behind Advanced inside More. A section header shows a one-line summary and a count of changed values. A shut tier that holds changes says so (“More · 2 changed”), so a changed value is never invisible.
  • Every action is in one pinned dock: ① Measure → ② Insert at measured point, side by side, then Insert at cursor, Update and Live. They are always visible, whatever you’ve scrolled to.
  • Search covers every parameter, nested ones included: all ~230 config keys, which come to 181 controls once the four door types share their common ones. It understands synonyms (sill, threshold, muntin, astragal, ironmongery, fanlight…), puts the real control in the result so you can edit it there, and has “Show in panel” to open the path. Parameters for other products are listed apart, with a switch button. Changed lists every value that differs from its default, each with its own reset.
  • Rows are compact and two to a line at the dialog’s 780 px. Counts use steppers and enums use segmented buttons. Dependent fields only appear when their switch is on.
  • Tier 1 is about one screen. At the default 780 × 1200 size, a casement’s Tier 1 fits with no scrolling (928 px of content in a 928 px body). Sliding sash needs about 110 px of scroll, and bifold and sliding doors 120–160 px. Single and double doors need about 350 px, because their handles now have their own Hardware section in Tier 1. Today it’s 2,155 and 3,137 px.

5Building it

  • Give every descriptor in the five UiSystem__Config__.js files a section and a tier. The door descriptors already have section, which nothing reads yet. One renderer then groups by section and tier, in place of the fixed <section>s in UiLayout.html.
  • The visibility helpers in na_onConfigChange stay as they are. A tier is just a wrapper that’s open or shut.
  • The product strip writes the existing keys (ui_element_category plus ui_window_type or ui_exterior_door_type), so saved elements and na_hydrateHierarchyFromModeFlags don’t change.
  • The dock moves #na-btn-measure, #na-btn-create-at-measurement, #na-btn-create, #na-btn-update and #na-btn-live into one sticky footer. The ids stay the same, so the bridges keep working.
  • Search builds its index at load from the same descriptors (label, id, section, tier) plus a small synonyms table, and draws inline controls with the same renderer.
  • The four door configs repeat the same nine fielded-panel controls. One shared set keyed by product prefix would remove three of the copies.
  • While you’re in there, hide the cill fields when Cill is off, and consider making Fuse Parts default on.
